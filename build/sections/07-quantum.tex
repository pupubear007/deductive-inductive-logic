\section{Application: a quantum information model}\label{sec:quantum}

This section gives a physical realization of the framework in quantum information theory. Thoughts
become subspaces of a Hilbert space, deduction becomes certain measurement and unitary evolution,
induction becomes quantum state tomography, and the binary and polarized foundation becomes qubits
and spins. The classical setting of \cref{sec:setting} reappears as the special case in which all
observables commute (\cref{prop:classical}). All propositions of this section are formalized in
Lean with Mathlib (\cref{app:formalization}); the physical implementations, Landauer's principle and
the experimental proposals are not.

Throughout, $\Hil$ is a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, a \emph{state} is a unit vector
$\ket v\in\Hil$ or a density operator $\rho$, and a \emph{projector} is an operator $P$ with
$P=P^\dagger=P^2$. For projectors, $P\le Q$ means $\operatorname{ran}P\subseteq\operatorname{ran}Q$,
equivalently $QP=P$. Physical time is written $\tau$, to keep $t$ for theories.

\subsection{Deduction as certain measurement}

A thought $\varphi$ is realized by a projector $P_\varphi$: the property ``the system lies in
$\operatorname{ran}P_\varphi$''. Entailment is subspace inclusion, and negative polarity is the
orthogonal complement, $P^{\down}=I-P$.

\begin{proposition}[Quantum deductive certainty]\label{prop:qsound}
Let $P_\varphi\le P_\psi$ and let $\ket v$ be a state with $P_\varphi\ket v=\ket v$. Then measuring
$\{P_\psi,\,I-P_\psi\}$ on $\ket v$ yields $P_\psi$ with probability
$\bra v P_\psi\ket v=1$. Moreover $I-P_\psi\le I-P_\varphi$.
\lean{quantum\_deduction\_sound}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $P_\psi P_\varphi=P_\varphi$, we get $P_\psi\ket v=P_\psi P_\varphi\ket v=P_\varphi\ket v=\ket v$,
so $\bra v P_\psi\ket v=\braket vv=1$. For the second claim,
$(I-P_\varphi)(I-P_\psi)=I-P_\psi-P_\varphi+P_\varphi P_\psi=I-P_\psi$, using
$P_\varphi P_\psi=(P_\psi P_\varphi)^\dagger=P_\varphi$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:qsound} is the quantum form of \cref{thm:sound} and \cref{prop:tollens}. Unitary
evolution is deductive in the same sense: $\ket v\mapsto U\ket v$ is deterministic.

\begin{proposition}[Unitary deduction]\label{prop:unitary}
Let $U^\dagger U=I$. If $UP_\varphi U^\dagger\le P_\psi$ and $P_\varphi\ket v=\ket v$, then
$P_\psi U\ket v=U\ket v$. Moreover $\Tr(U\rho U^\dagger\,UPU^\dagger)=\Tr(\rho P)$ for all $\rho,P$.
\lean{unitary\_deduction, born\_unitary\_conj}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$U\ket v=UP_\varphi\ket v=(UP_\varphi U^\dagger)U\ket v$, and $P_\psi(UP_\varphi U^\dagger)=UP_\varphi U^\dagger$.
The second claim is cyclicity of the trace.
\end{proof}

A general measurement is not certain. For a projective measurement $\{P_n\}$ with
$\sum_n P_n=I$ and $P_nP_m=\delta_{nm}P_n$, the Born rule gives
\[
  \ket v\ \longmapsto\ \frac{P_n\ket v}{\lVert P_n\ket v\rVert}
  \quad\text{with probability}\quad p(n)=\bra vP_n\ket v .
\]
What is deduced from the state is the distribution $p$, not the individual outcome. For a spin,
$\ket v=\alpha\ket\up+\beta\ket\down$ yields $\ket\up$ with probability $|\alpha|^2$ and
$\ket\down$ with probability $|\beta|^2$; certainty is the case $|\alpha|=1$ or $|\beta|=1$.

\begin{proposition}[The classical framework is the commuting case]\label{prop:classical}
Let $\{\ket n\}_{n\in\W}$ be an orthonormal basis indexed by the finite set of worlds $\W$, and set
$P_\varphi=\sum_{n:\,\varphi(n)}\ket n\!\bra n$. Then
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $P_\varphi\le P_\psi$ if and only if $\varphi\models\psi$, and $P_{\neg\varphi}=I-P_\varphi$;
        \lean{classicalProj\_le\_iff, classicalProj\_not}
  \item if $\rho=\sum_n p_n\ket n\!\bra n$ with every $p_n>0$, then for a study
        $s=(o_1,\dots,o_k)$ and theory $t$,
        $\Tr\bigl(\rho\,P_t P_{o_1}\cdots P_{o_k}\bigr)>0$ if and only if $s\circlearrowright t$.
        \lean{classical\_trace\_pos\_iff}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) $\operatorname{ran}P_\varphi$ is spanned by the $\ket n$ with $\varphi(n)$, and such a basis vector
lies in $\operatorname{ran}P_\psi$ exactly when $\psi(n)$. The second claim holds because every
basis vector satisfies exactly one of $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$.
(2) The projectors are diagonal, so their product is the projector onto the $\ket n$ with
$t(n)\wedge o_1(n)\wedge\dots\wedge o_k(n)$, and the trace is $\sum p_n$ over those $n$. A sum of
positive numbers is positive exactly when it is non-empty, which is \cref{def:support}.
\end{proof}

Part~(2) realizes \cref{rem:probability} physically. Outside the commuting case the projectors
of $\Hil$ do not form a Boolean algebra~\cite{birkhoff,vonneumann}. For a spin, with $P_{x+}$ the
projector onto $(\ket\up+\ket\down)/\sqrt2$,
\[
  P_{x+}\wedge(P_{z+}\vee P_{z-})=P_{x+}\wedge I=P_{x+},
  \qquad
  (P_{x+}\wedge P_{z+})\vee(P_{x+}\wedge P_{z-})=0,
\]
so the distributive law fails.

\begin{proposition}[Quantum logic is not distributive]\label{prop:nondistrib}
In the lattice of subspaces of $\mathbb C^2$, with $Z_\pm$ spanned by $\ket\up,\ket\down$ and $X_+$
by $\ket\up+\ket\down$: $X_+\wedge(Z_+\vee Z_-)=X_+\ne0$ while
$(X_+\wedge Z_+)\vee(X_+\wedge Z_-)=0$.
\lean{quantum\_not\_distributive}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$Z_+\vee Z_-=\mathbb C^2$. A vector $c(1,1)=d(a,b)$ with $a\ne b$ forces $c=0$, so both meets are $0$.
\end{proof}

This marks where the quantum model goes beyond
\cref{sec:deduction}: the identity of \cref{prop:imp} relies on a Boolean structure that
incompatible observables lack.

\subsection{Induction as state tomography}

In the quantum model a study is a record of outcomes $n_1,\dots,n_N$ obtained by measuring
identical copies of an unknown state, and a theory is a state $\rho$ that explains them. The
likelihood of $\rho$ is $L_N(\rho)=\prod_{k=1}^N\Tr(\rho P_{n_k})$, and $\rho$ is \emph{supported}
when $L_N(\rho)>0$. Maximum-likelihood tomography selects
\[
  \hat\rho_{\mathrm{ML}}=\arg\max_\rho\ \prod_{k=1}^N\Tr(\rho P_{n_k}),
\]
and adaptive tomography chooses the next basis from the outcomes so far,
\[
  h_{k+1}=f(n_1,\dots,n_k;\,h_1,\dots,h_k).
\]
This is the circular arrow $s\exists\circlearrowright
t\varphi$ with iteration $i$ indexing the rounds.

\begin{proposition}[Quantum refinement, falsification and underdetermination]\label{prop:qhume}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Each further outcome can only shrink the set of supported states:
        $L_{N+1}(\rho)>0$ implies $L_N(\rho)>0$.
        \lean{QSupported.of\_concat}
  \item Suppose $N$ measurements of $\{\ket\up\!\bra\up,\ket\down\!\bra\down\}$ all give $\up$.
        Then $\ket\up$ is the maximum-likelihood state, yet every state
        $\ket{\theta,\phi}=\cos\theta\ket\up+e^{i\phi}\sin\theta\ket\down$ with $0\le\theta<\pi/2$
        is supported, with likelihood $\cos^{2N}\theta>0$.
        \lean{spinUp\_maxLikelihood, spinState\_supported}
  \item A single outcome $\down$ refutes the theory $\ket\up$: its likelihood becomes $0$.
        \lean{spinUp\_refuted}
  \item No number of measurements in this basis distinguishes $\ket{\theta,\phi}$ from
        $\ket{\theta,\phi'}$: their outcome statistics are equal for all $\phi,\phi'$.
        \lean{likelihood\_spinState\_phase}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) $L_{N+1}(\rho)=L_N(\rho)\Tr(\rho P_{n_{N+1}})$, and every factor $\Tr(\rho P)$ lies in
$[0,1]$, so $0\le L_{N+1}(\rho)\le L_N(\rho)$.
(2) $\bra{\theta,\phi}\,\up\rangle\langle\up\,\ket{\theta,\phi}=\cos^2\theta$, so the likelihood is
$\cos^{2N}\theta$, which is positive for $\theta<\pi/2$ and largest at $\theta=0$.
(3) $\bra\up\,\down\rangle\langle\down\,\ket\up=0$.
(4) The outcome probabilities are $\cos^2\theta$ and $\sin^2\theta$, independent of $\phi$.
\end{proof}

Parts (1)--(3) are the quantum counterparts of \cref{thm:mono}, \cref{thm:hume} and
\cref{prop:popper}. Part~(4) is a limitation with no classical counterpart: data from one basis
leave the phase undetermined however much of it is collected, so a tomographically complete set
of bases is needed. In the language of the framework, the hypothesis $h$ (the basis) bounds what
any study can reveal.

\subsection{Foundation: qubits and spins}

The foundation map $m$ of \cref{sec:foundation} is realized by the projector onto $\ket b$ in the
basis selected by $h$: state $1$ affirms and state $0$ negates, as in \cref{prop:m}. A register of
$n$ qubits with spin degrees of freedom has states
\[
  \ket v=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}\ \sum_{\sigma\in\{\up,\down\}^n} c_{x,\sigma}\ket x\ket\sigma ,
\]
and iteration is a circuit of depth $d$, $\ket{v_{\mathrm{final}}}=U_d\cdots U_2U_1\ket{v_{\mathrm{initial}}}$.

\subsection{Physical implementations}

\paragraph{Deduction: quantum gates.} Single-qubit gates ($X$, $Y$, $Z$, Hadamard) and two-qubit
gates (CNOT, CZ) act as $\ket v\mapsto U\ket v$, deterministic transformations that realize the
straight arrow~\cite{nielsen,deutsch}.

\paragraph{Induction: variational algorithms.} The variational quantum eigensolver and the quantum
approximate optimization algorithm prepare $\ket{v(\theta)}=U(\theta)\ket0$ and update
\[
  \theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\eta\,\nabla_\theta\bra{v(\theta_k)}\hat H\ket{v(\theta_k)},
\]
with the gradient estimated from measurement outcomes. The parameters are the theory, the
measurements are the study, and each update is one turn of the circular arrow~\cite{preskill}.

\paragraph{Induction: quantum machine learning.} Training data $\{(x_k,y_k)\}$ form the study, a
parametrized circuit $U(\theta)$ is the theory, and training minimizes
$L(\theta)=\sum_k\bigl|y_k-\bra{v(x_k)}M\ket{v(x_k)}\bigr|^2$ for an observable
$M$~\cite{biamonte}.

\subsection{Thermodynamic reading}

Information has a physical cost. Recording a study of $N$ binary outcomes uses $N$ bits, and by
Landauer's principle resetting that record for reuse dissipates at least $N k_B T\ln 2$ of
heat~\cite{landauer,bennett}. The two arrows then differ thermodynamically:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Deduction preserves entropy.} Unitary evolution leaves the von Neumann entropy
        $S(\rho)=-\Tr\rho\ln\rho=-\sum_k\lambda_k\ln\lambda_k$ unchanged, $S(U\rho U^\dagger)=S(\rho)$,
        because $U\rho U^\dagger$ and $\rho$ have the same characteristic polynomial; no
        information is gained or lost.
        \lean{vonNeumannEntropy\_unitary\_conj}
  \item \emph{Induction lowers the observer's entropy, at a price.} Observation reduces the
        expected Shannon entropy of the observer's belief about the theory,
        $H(T\mid S)\le H(T)$. As with Maxwell's demon, the reduction is paid for by the
        Landauer cost of erasing the record~\cite{bennett}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Noise}\label{sec:noise}

Real devices are noisy. A simple model is the depolarizing channel
$\mathcal D_\varepsilon(\rho)=(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon I/d$ on a $d$-dimensional system.

\begin{proposition}[Deduction and falsification under noise]\label{prop:noise}
Let $0\le\varepsilon$. If $\Tr(\rho P)=1$ for a projector $P$, then
$\Tr(\mathcal D_\varepsilon(\rho)P)\ge1-\varepsilon$. For a spin and $\varepsilon>0$,
$\Tr\bigl(\mathcal D_\varepsilon(\ket\up\!\bra\up)\,\ket\down\!\bra\down\bigr)=\varepsilon/2>0$.
\lean{born\_depolarize\_ge, noisy\_spinUp\_not\_refuted}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Tr(\mathcal D_\varepsilon(\rho)P)=(1-\varepsilon)\Tr(\rho P)+\varepsilon\Tr(P)/d$ and $\Tr P\ge0$. For
the spin, $\Tr(\ket\up\!\bra\up\,\ket\down\!\bra\down)=0$ and $\Tr(\ket\down\!\bra\down)/2=1/2$.
\end{proof}

Noise thus weakens both arrows in a controlled way: deductive certainty degrades by at most the
noise rate, and falsification (\cref{prop:qhume}(3)) becomes statistical, since a single outcome
$\down$ no longer refutes $\ket\up$. Error correction is the means of restoring them.

\subsection{Experimental proposals}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Tomography.} Prepare an unknown state $\ket v$, measure in a tomographically complete
        set of bases, reconstruct $\hat\rho$ by maximum likelihood, and report the fidelity
        $F=\bra v\hat\rho\ket v$. Plotting $F$ against $N$ shows inductive refinement directly.
  \item \emph{Quantum walk.} The state $\ket{v(\tau)}=\sum_n\alpha_n(\tau)\ket n\ket{c_n}$, with
        position $\ket n$ and coin $\ket{c_n}$, displays iteration as coherent evolution.
  \item \emph{Error correction.} A logical qubit $\ket{v_L}$ is a protected thought, the noisy
        physical qubits are existence, the syndrome measurement is the study $s\exists$, and the
        decoder's inferred error is the theory $t\varphi$; applying the correction is then a
        deduction.
\end{enumerate}
