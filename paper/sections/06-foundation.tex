\section{The foundation}\label{sec:foundation}

\begin{definition}[Foundation map]
Let $I$, $H$, $L$ be sets of iterations, hypotheses and levels, and let
$\beta:I\times H\times L\to\Phi$ assign a base thought to each index. The \emph{foundation map}
$m:I\times H\times L\times\{0,1\}\to\Phi$ is
\[
  m(i,h,l,1)=\beta(i,h,l),\qquad m(i,h,l,0)=\neg\beta(i,h,l).
\]
\end{definition}

Thus state $1$ carries polarity $\up$ and state $0$ carries polarity $\down$. Since existences
are thoughts (\cref{sec:setting}), $m$ takes values in $\Phi$.

\begin{proposition}[The binary state is the truth value]\label{prop:m}
For every world $w$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$, $m(i,h,l,b)(w)$ holds if and only if $\beta(i,h,l)(w)$
agrees with $b$, that is, $\beta(i,h,l)(w)\iff b=1$.
\lean{m\_spec}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $b=1$ both sides say $\beta(i,h,l)(w)$. For $b=0$ both sides say $\neg\beta(i,h,l)(w)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ordered levels}\label{sec:levels}

The indices $i,h,l$ are uninterpreted. We now order the levels, reading lower levels as
observational and higher ones as theoretical.

\begin{definition}[Hierarchy]\label{def:hierarchy}
Let $L$ be preordered. A \emph{hierarchy} assigns a thought $\beta_l$ to each level so that
$l\le l'$ implies $\beta_{l'}\models\beta_l$: a theory entails its observable consequences. Given
a study $s$, level $l$ is \emph{established} if $s\models\beta_l$ and \emph{tenable} if
$s\circlearrowright\beta_l$.
\lean{Hierarchy}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Iteration moves upward]\label{thm:upward}
Established and tenable levels form down-sets. If every observation of $s$ occurs in $s'$, every
level established by $s$ is established by $s'$, and every level tenable given $s'$ is tenable
given $s$. If $s$ is satisfiable, every established level is tenable.
\lean{Hierarchy.Established.mono}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Down-sets: entailment is transitive, and a world satisfying $\beta_{l'}$ satisfies $\beta_l$. A
world satisfying $s'$ satisfies $s$, so $s\models\beta_l$ gives $s'\models\beta_l$; the tenable case
is \cref{thm:mono}. A world satisfying $s$ then satisfies every established $\beta_l$.
\end{proof}

Iteration therefore raises the floor of established levels and lowers the ceiling of tenable
ones, with the floor below the ceiling as long as the study is consistent. Along an iteration
$i\mapsto s_i$ that only adds observations, a level established at step $i$ stays established at
every later step (\texttt{Hierarchy.Established.iterate}).
