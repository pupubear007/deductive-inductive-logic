\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Deductive logic derives particular conclusions from general premises with certainty: all men are
mortal; Socrates is a man; therefore Socrates is mortal~\cite{aristotle}. Inductive logic builds
general theories from particular observations with only tentative support: many swans have been
seen to be white, so perhaps all swans are white. Textual arguments and static diagrams, such as
Venn diagrams and Peirce's existential graphs~\cite{peirce}, show the structure of a single
inference well but do not show reasoning as a process in which deduction and induction alternate.

This paper proposes a diagram (\cref{fig:framework}) that places both modes of reasoning in one
picture, with a straight arrow for deduction, a circular arrow for induction, and a foundation of
iterations, hypotheses, levels, binary states and polarities. We then give the diagram a precise
semantics and prove what it asserts.

\paragraph{Related work.} Diagrams as rigorous logical representations go back to Peirce and
have been given formal semantics, for example for Venn diagrams by Shin~\cite{shin}; our diagram
differs in depicting two modes of inference and their interaction rather than the content of a
single argument. The semantics of \cref{sec:setting} is the standard possible-worlds
reading of propositions~\cite{kripke}. Our qualitative notion of support is the consistency
requirement underlying confirmation theory~\cite{hempel}, and its quantitative refinement in
\cref{sec:confirmation} is Bayesian incremental confirmation~\cite{carnap,earman,fitelson}.
Falsification follows Popper~\cite{popper}, and the treatment of defeasible inference in
\cref{sec:defeasible} uses the preferential semantics of Kraus, Lehmann and
Magidor~\cite{klm,makinson}. The quantum model draws on quantum logic~\cite{birkhoff,dallachiara}
and quantum state tomography~\cite{hradil,nielsen}. The plant-pathology application draws on
quantitative work on diagnosis, forecasting and decision-making in plant disease
epidemiology~\cite{hughes1999,madden2007,willbur2018}. Machine-checked formalization of philosophical
arguments has been pursued with automated and interactive provers~\cite{fitelsonzalta,benzmuller};
we use Lean~4 and Mathlib~\cite{lean4,mathlib}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A diagrammatic framework for deduction $\varphi\to\exists$ and induction
        $s\exists\circlearrowright t\varphi$ (\cref{sec:framework}).
  \item A possible-worlds semantics for every symbol of the diagram (\cref{sec:setting}).
  \item Theorems for each mode: soundness, transitivity and modus tollens for deduction
        (\cref{sec:deduction}); monotone refinement, falsification and the failure of
        induction as deduction (\cref{thm:hume}) for induction (\cref{sec:induction}); and a
        truth-value characterization of the foundation (\cref{sec:foundation}).
  \item An application to quantum information theory (\cref{sec:quantum}): a dictionary from the
        framework to quantum states, measurements and Hamiltonians; quantum counterparts of the
        deductive and inductive theorems (\cref{prop:qsound,prop:qhume}); the classical
        framework as the commuting case (\cref{prop:classical}); and physical implementations,
        a thermodynamic reading and experimental proposals.
  \item An application to plant disease diagnosis and forecasting (\cref{sec:plant}): an
        assay-resolution theorem (\cref{thm:resolves}) that gives the hypothesis index $h$ a
        concrete reading, Koch's postulates as hypothetico-deductive confirmation
        (\cref{prop:koch}), and diagnostic tests and forecasts as confirmation and defeasible
        inference (\cref{prop:plantconfirm,prop:sclerotinia}).
  \item Three extensions: Bayesian confirmation as the quantitative inductive polarity
        (\cref{sec:confirmation}), defeasible inference (\cref{sec:defeasible}), and ordered
        levels (\cref{sec:levels}).
  \item A machine-checked formalization of every theorem of
        Sections~\ref{sec:deduction}--\ref{sec:plant} in Lean~4~\cite{lean4} and
        Mathlib~\cite{mathlib} (Appendix~\ref{app:formalization}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Outline.} \Cref{sec:framework} presents the diagram and \cref{sec:setting} its
semantics. \Cref{sec:deduction,sec:induction,sec:foundation} prove the results for deduction,
induction and the foundation, including the extensions. \Cref{sec:quantum} develops the quantum
model and \cref{sec:plant} the application to plant pathology, \cref{sec:discussion} discusses the results and their limitations, and
Appendix~\ref{app:formalization} maps every result to its Lean declaration.
