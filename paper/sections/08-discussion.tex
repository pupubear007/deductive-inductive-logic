\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Philosophy.} The straight arrow carries Descartes' movement from thought to
existence~\cite{descartes}: what is entailed holds in every world where the premises hold
(\cref{thm:sound}). The circular arrow carries Hume's and Popper's view of induction: a
generalization is supported, never entailed (\cref{thm:hume}), and can be overturned by a single
counterexample (\cref{prop:popper}). Levels $l$ in the foundation give a place for Kant's
hierarchical structuring of thought~\cite{kant}.

\paragraph{Quantum analogies.} The quantum model of \cref{sec:quantum} suggests three further
analogies, which we state as analogies rather than results.
\emph{Complementarity}: as quantum objects show wave and particle aspects, reasoning involves both
abstract thought ($\varphi$) and concrete existence ($\exists$).
\emph{Uncertainty}: incompatible observables cannot both be sharp, which is a physical limitation on
what any study can reveal, distinct from Hume's logical one (\cref{prop:qhume}(4)).
\emph{Entanglement}: entangled systems show correlations that no classical model reproduces,
suggesting that logical relations between parts of a system need not reduce to relations between
the parts; these correlations cannot be used to signal, so they do not conflict with causality.
These views connect to the idea that physical processes are computations~\cite{deutsch,lloyd}.

\paragraph{Education and cognition.} The top row of the diagram is suitable for a first course in
logic; the foundation invites discussion of polarity and iteration. The visual layout follows
the view that diagrams make inferential structure explicit~\cite{larkin,johnsonlaird}.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Infinite sets of worlds.} The confirmation results of \cref{sec:confirmation} assume
        finitely many worlds. For infinite or continuous sets of worlds the same definitions can be
        stated with measures and conditional measures, and we expect
        \cref{thm:hd,prop:ascend} to carry over for theories of positive prior probability. The
        sign of confirmation does not depend on the prior (\cref{cor:bswan}); its size does.
  \item \emph{Hypotheses and iterations.} Levels are now ordered (\cref{sec:levels}), but the
        hypothesis index $h$ remains uninterpreted. A natural reading lets $h$ index a partition of
        $\W$, the distinctions a study under $h$ can draw, so that a theory is identifiable under
        $h$ only if it is a union of cells. \Cref{prop:qhume}(4) is the quantum instance.
  \item \emph{Higher-order logic.} Every theorem is proved for an arbitrary type of worlds,
        including function spaces: in the swan examples a world is a predicate on birds, so $\All$
        already quantifies over a predicate. A higher-order logic with its own syntax and proof
        system is not treated; Henkin semantics~\cite{henkin}, with soundness proved against the
        possible-worlds reading, is the natural route.
  \item \emph{Physical realization.} The quantum model is limited in practice by decoherence,
        state-preparation and measurement errors, and scale~\cite{preskill,arute}.
        \Cref{prop:noise} quantifies the effect of depolarizing noise on both arrows. Showing that
        the three-qubit repetition code restores deductive certainty to order $\varepsilon^2$ is a
        next step; error-corrected implementations remain experimental work.
  \item \emph{Thermodynamics.} Landauer's principle is a physical law, assumed rather than
        derived. The inequality $H(T\mid S)\le H(T)$ is a standard consequence of the concavity of
        entropy but is not formalized here.
  \item \emph{Learners.} Whether the diagram helps learners is an empirical question. We propose a
        preregistered randomized trial: students in an introductory logic course receive a lesson
        built on the diagram or the same lesson in prose, and are tested on classifying arguments
        as deductive or inductive, on recognizing fallacies (affirming the consequent, hasty
        generalization) and on predicting the effect of a counterexample, with a retention test
        four weeks later. Detecting a medium effect ($d=0.5$) with power $0.8$ at $\alpha=0.05$
        requires about $64$ students per arm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

We have given a diagram for deduction and induction, a possible-worlds semantics for its symbols,
and machine-checked theorems for what it depicts: deduction is certain and transitive, negative
polarity is modus tollens, iteration narrows the supported theories, one counterexample falsifies,
and no finite study entails a universal theory. Bayesian confirmation makes the ascending
probability of the circular arrow precise: a theory is confirmed by every genuine test it
predicts, more so as the tests accumulate, yet never entailed. Defeasible inference sits between
the two arrows, and ordered levels show iteration moving upward. Realized in quantum information
theory, the same structure appears as certain measurement and unitary evolution on one side and
state tomography on the other, with the classical framework as the commuting case and with a
physical limit on induction that has no classical counterpart. This suggests that quantum
mechanics offers a natural physical setting for the interplay of deduction and induction.
